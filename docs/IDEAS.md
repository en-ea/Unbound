## Kills, carcasses and bait (30 Sep, from "what if a wolf kill stayed there")
First version built on camera-test (docs/SYSTEMS.md "Hunting"). Still open: shoulder-carrying small animals, snares
and bait items, feeding companions, shrine offerings, repeat village requests, NPCs physically carrying things off.
- **Carcasses stay** after a kill (big animals). Small animals keep instant drops.
- **Carve on the spot** (quick, basic meat and hide) **or take the whole body** (better pay, rare parts:
  clean pelts, tusks, trophies).
- **Carrying:** small on the shoulder, big dragged, huge only by cart or horse. While carrying you are slow and
  can't roll or fight; drop it to defend yourself (a wolf may grab it meanwhile). Some animals only
  transportable certain ways.
- **Scavengers** smell it: crows first, then wolves; they can fight over it. **Rot:** worth less over time, then gone.
- **NPCs can take it too**, whether you care or not. More broadly: **anyone can take things left lying around.**
- **Bait:** some rare creatures only come if lured (bait + spot + wait + ambush; snares). **Three carcasses in a
  clearing at night summon a boss.**
- **Uses:** trophies at home (especially rare kills), feeding pets and companions, shrine offerings, village
  requests ("bring a boar for the feast", now and then), selling fresh kills to a butcher.
- Tech note: cap how many carcasses exist at once (good practice on any device, a must on the web build).

## Homes you can enter, and villagers who live there (1 Oct)
- **A good number of houses can be entered** eventually, not only your own.
- **Some are locked: you have to break in** (pick the lock, a window, sneak at night). Ties into sneaking and theft.
- **Each named villager has a real home** that stays the same and makes sense: where they sleep, eat and work,
  their own things inside (links to "NPCs have a real inventory" above). They go home at night.
