# The Tales of Wenbrook, years 1-1000

*Told from the village's own memory: every name, cause and turn below happened in the simulation (seed 1000); only the sentence patterns were written by hand.*

## Year 37: The Burning of Lettice

That season people saw a stillborn foal, and began to watch one another. Whispers started that Lettice had cursed the village - charms of twisted straw found by a doorway. Wat named Lettice before the elder. Godric, the elder, heard it in the square. They put Lettice in the millpond. They floated. Guilty. The sentence was the fire. They burned Lettice at the stake at dusk; the smoke was seen from the next valley. A few years later, Wat stood up in the shrine and took it all back: there had been no curse, only fear, and they had named Lettice for it. The truth came too late for Lettice. Now there are candles on Lettice's grave, and strangers come to kneel. They are remembered as Lettice the Blessed.

## Year 60: The Empty Cell

One evening old grudges came to blows: Maud struck Oswin. Tam named Maud before the elder. Tam, the elder, judged it too. The sentence was exile. Simkin had never forgiven Tam for what was done to Maud. One evening a fight at the shrine went too far: Simkin struck Tam, and Tam did not get up. Piers named Simkin before the elder. Edric, the elder, heard it in the square. The sentence was the rope. But on the night before, Simkin's cell was found empty and the ropes cut. Everyone knew it was Gwen, and no one said so. Simkin was gone by morning, into the woods. They are remembered as Simkin Who Walked Out and Gwen the Brave.

## Year 192: The Stones That Were Not Thrown

That was a hungry year. Edric, with the children crying for bread, took a goose from the Thatcher house while they were at work. Cecily came to Sella: it was Edric. Gwen found an empty goose pen, a squawk remembered. Sella named Edric before the elder. Sybil, the elder, heard it in the square. The sentence was exile. They put Edric in the exile. But the crowd closed round the condemned instead of the stones, and the elder let them go.

## Year 221: Lettice Walks Out

Then came a hailstorm in summer. The old women said it meant a curse among them. Whispers started that Lettice had cursed the village - charms of twisted straw found by a doorway. Orla named Lettice before the elder. Tam, the elder, heard it in the square. They put Lettice in the millpond. They floated. Guilty. The law asked for the fire, but the elder, weary of blood, gave exile. Lettice walked out of the south gate with one bundle and did not look back. A few years later, Orla stood up in the shrine and took it all back: there had been no curse, only fear, and they had named Lettice for it. The truth came out. They sent riders after Lettice, and Lettice came home through the south gate, thinner and older; the village came out to meet them, ashamed. They are remembered as Lettice Who Came Home.

## Year 245: The Offering of Godric

That was the year the storm came down on the Cooper house. When the air cleared, the family was gone, and in their place stood people in furs who spoke like the old songs - the Coopers' own forebears. The forebears read the strange world as their gods' anger. At dusk they took Godric to the stake, and the drums began. At dawn Ysa made the offering, as their fathers had.
