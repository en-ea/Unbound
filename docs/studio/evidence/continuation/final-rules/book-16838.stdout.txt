# The Tales of Ashcombe, years 1-1000

*Told from the village's own memory: every name, cause and turn below happened in the simulation (seed 16838); only the sentence patterns were written by hand.*

## Year 15: Rolf Walks Out

One evening old grudges came to blows: Colm struck Gwen. Merric named Colm before the elder. Tobin, the elder, heard it in the square. The sentence was exile. Jory had never forgiven Merric for what was done to Colm. One evening old grudges came to blows: Jory struck Merric. Merric named Jory before the elder. Tobin, the elder, heard it in the square. The sentence was exile. Rolf had never forgiven Merric for what was done to Jory. One evening old grudges came to blows: Rolf struck Merric. Cecily named Rolf before the elder. Tobin, the elder, heard it in the square. Rolf confessed, and was spared the worst. The sentence was exile. Rolf walked out of the south gate with one bundle and did not look back. From that day the Hale and the Wendmoor would not share a well. They are remembered as Rolf Who Walked Out.

## Year 52: Kesh in the Pillory

One evening old grudges came to blows: Dagny struck Edith. Rafe named Dagny before the elder. Hugh, the elder, heard it in the square. The law asked for exile, but the elder, weary of blood, gave a day in the pillory. Dagny stood a day in the pillory while the village muttered. Kesh had never forgiven Rafe for what was done to Dagny. One evening old grudges came to blows: Kesh struck Rafe. Edith named Kesh before the elder. Hugh, the elder, heard it in the square. The law asked for exile, but the elder, weary of blood, gave a day in the pillory. They pelted Kesh with mud in the pillory until dusk. They are remembered as Kesh of the Pillory and Dagny of the Pillory.

## Year 88: The Stones That Were Not Thrown

That was a hungry year. While others starved, Maud's barn stayed full - full sacks glimpsed through a barn door in a hungry year. Alys named Maud before the elder. Alder, the elder, heard it in the square. They put Maud in the millpond. They floated. Guilty. The sentence was a day in the pillory. They put Maud in the pillory. But the crowd closed round the condemned instead of the stones, and the elder let them go.

## Year 149: The Offering of Elsbet

That was the year the storm came down on the Fenwick house. When the air cleared, the family was gone, and in their place stood people in furs who spoke like the old songs - the Fenwicks' own forebears. The forebears read the strange world as their gods' anger. At dusk they took Elsbet to the stake, and the drums began. At dawn Kell made the offering, as their fathers had. Wystan named Kell before the elder. Bettony, the elder, heard it in the square. Kell confessed, and was spared the worst. The sentence was the rope. But before the day came, the mist rolled back over the loaf house, and Kell was gone with it, back to their own time. They are remembered as Kell Who Confessed.

## Year 173: Osric the Branded

That was a hungry year. Osric, half-starved, took grain from the Wendmoor house while they were at work. Bram found the grain sacks lighter than yesterday. Bram named Osric before the elder. Oswin, the elder, heard it in the square. Osric confessed, and was spared the worst. The sentence was the brand. They branded Osric in the square, and everyone smelled it. They are remembered as Osric the Branded.

## Year 182: The Empty Cell

Ilse was found dead on the south road. Wystan found crows over a still shape. Wystan named Rafe before the elder. Ansel, the elder, heard it in the square. The sentence was the rope. But on the night before, Rafe's cell was found empty and the ropes cut. Everyone knew it was Joan, and no one said so. Rafe was gone by morning, into the woods. They are remembered as Rafe Who Walked Out and Joan the Brave.

## Year 634: The Burning of Gwen

Then came a stillborn foal. The old women said it meant a curse among them. Whispers started that Gwen had cursed the village - charms of twisted straw found by a doorway. Ansel named Gwen before the elder. Garrow, the elder, heard it in the square. They put Gwen in the millpond. They floated. Guilty. The sentence was the fire. They burned Gwen at the stake at dusk; the smoke was seen from the next valley. Before the month was out, Ansel stood up in the shrine and took it all back: there had been no curse, only fear, and they had named Gwen for it. The truth came too late for Gwen. Now there are candles on Gwen's grave, and strangers come to kneel. They are remembered as Gwen the Blessed.
